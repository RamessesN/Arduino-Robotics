\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}
We measured three sets of data every 40cm within the range of 0 to 400cm,
calculated the average of the three sets of data, and plotted them into an average radar chart, as shown in appendix.

By integrating the data from angles ranging from 0 to 180 degrees and distances ranging from 0 to 400cm,
we have created the following radar chart (Figure~\ref{fig:heatmap}).
It illustrates how far obstacles can be detected at different angles when the forward linear distance is the same.

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.8\linewidth]{../Photos/heatmap Medium}
  \rule{35em}{0.5pt}
  \caption{A distribution heatmap divided every 40cm within the range of 0 to 400cm}
  \label{fig:heatmap}
\end{figure}

Taking all the data into account, we have integrated the data from 0 to 180 degrees and 0 to 400cm, and
created the following radar chart (Figure~\ref{fig:sector-radar-chart}) to illustrate the maximum distance at which obstacles can be detected at each angle.
